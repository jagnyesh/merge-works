What we're trying to achieve

A merge is the lock-on. Until the crane lifts it, a car is a reversible proposal on its own lane. The crane is the one irreversible step — the commit becomes shared history everyone branches from. And the trunk moves while you work: every merge forces the other in-flight cars through the fitter (rebase) before their own lock-on. That reconciliation is the tax parallel work pays.

Now the loop closes. The Plan hopper keeps the queue topped to lanes×2 (§10 queue health), stamping each intent at its tier weight — a high-risk car carries a tall ADR stack, a low-risk one a thin slip. Every merge pulls the next intent. And the Maintain monitor turns a post-merge signal — nightly CI, an eval regression, cost drift, a LangSmith trace — into a fresh intent.md that re-enters the front of the queue. That is the course's “a production metric leaving its range becomes a new intent.md that restarts planning.”

Honest adaptation: you have no production deploy yet (§11), so the return-pipe draws from post-merge signals, not a prod environment. Same loop, moved one boundary upstream.

The ceiling isn't the machine — it's the beam. Pull Lanes to 4–5. Builds still go green (open→green holds), but green→merge balloons and review debt climbs: one reviewer covers WIP 2. Faster models just feed more cars into that same queue. So the target isn't lanes running, Plan churning, or signals firing — it's verified merges per week, and you add a lane only while the beam still reaches every green car.
